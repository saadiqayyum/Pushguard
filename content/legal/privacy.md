## Who we are

The hosted Pushguard service and this website are operated by **Pushguard** ("we", "us"). This policy explains what we collect when you use them and what we do with it.

Pushguard's source code is published under the MIT license. If you run that code yourself, this policy does not cover your installation.

## What we collect

### When you sign in

You sign in with GitHub. GitHub shares your username, name, email address and profile picture with us. We save your username and your organization roles. Your name, email address and picture are kept only in your browser while you are signed in, and are not saved by us.

When you sign in, we also check with GitHub which organizations you belong to and your role in each.

### When you install Pushguard

- The account or organization it is installed on, and who installed it.
- Organization members, their roles, teams, and who can access which repository, as GitHub reports them.
- The names, branches and settings of the repositories you install it on.

### The changes we review

We read the changes pushed to those repositories and their pull requests. For each alert we keep what is needed to explain it: the repository, branch and commit, the file names and the short excerpts that raised it, and the GitHub username and email address of the person who pushed.

To review new changes in context, we keep a list of names that appear in your code, such as file and function names. We do not store copies of your source files.

### Alerts and settings

- Alerts and their history: status changes, assignments, and comments added to them.
- Scans you run and their results.
- Settings you configure, such as rules, who to notify, and AI review options.
- AI keys you add. They are encrypted when stored, decrypted only when a review runs, and shown back to you only as a short hint.
- Alert destination addresses you add, such as a Slack or Discord webhook. They are encrypted when stored.
- Access tokens you create to connect your own tools. We keep only enough to recognize them, not the tokens themselves.

### Activity records

We keep records of activity on the service to run and secure it. They can include your GitHub username, organization and repository names, and errors.

## Cookies and local storage

We use cookies only to make the service work:

- A sign-in cookie that keeps you signed in, and short-lived cookies used while you sign in, including one that carries your acceptance of the Terms of Service to your account.
- Preference cookies that remember the organization you selected and the state of the sidebar and setup steps.
- A short-lived cookie that remembers a repository you asked to scan before signing in.

Your light or dark theme choice is saved in your browser. We do not use analytics, advertising or tracking cookies, and this site loads no third-party tracking scripts.

## How we use it

- To review changes in the repositories you chose and raise alerts about them.
- To file alerts as issues in your repositories and send them to the destinations you set up.
- To show the dashboard to the people GitHub says may see it.
- To keep the service secure and to prevent abuse.

We do not sell or rent your information, and we do not use it for advertising. We use service providers to host and run Pushguard, and they process information only on our behalf. When you add your own AI key or alert destination, the relevant content goes to the provider or destination you chose. A list of our service providers is available on request at isaadqayyum@gmail.com.

## Why we are allowed to use it

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, we rely on these legal bases:

- To provide the service you or your organization asked for, which needs the information described above.
- Our legitimate interest in keeping the service and your repositories secure and in preventing abuse. This covers alerts that name the person who pushed a change, because a security alert is not useful without it.
- To meet legal obligations, where the law requires us to keep or share information.

## Where it is processed

Your information may be stored and processed in countries other than your own, including the United States. When we transfer information out of the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, we use safeguards recognized by law, such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

## AI review

AI review runs only if an organization owner adds their own AI key. When it runs, the relevant parts of the change are sent to the AI provider the owner chose, using the owner's key. That provider handles the content under its own terms and privacy policy.

We also use AI of our own to help our team research new attacks. It reads public security advisories and incident write-ups, and it is never given your code or your alerts.

## How long we keep it

We keep your information while Pushguard is installed. When you uninstall it, we delete your organization's information right away: repositories, members and access records, alerts and their history, scan results, rules, settings, the index of file names, AI keys and usage, alert destinations and tokens. The record that your GitHub login accepted the Terms of Service is kept, because it belongs to you, not the organization. Alerts already filed as issues in your repositories stay on GitHub, where you control them. You can also ask us to delete your information at any time by writing to isaadqayyum@gmail.com.

Activity records are kept for 30 days. You can delete your own AI keys, alert destinations and tokens from the dashboard at any time.

## How we protect it

AI keys and alert destination addresses are encrypted when stored. Access follows your GitHub roles: people see an organization's alerts only for repositories GitHub says they can read, and only organization owners can change rules and settings. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise that your information will never be accessed without permission.

## Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your information, to correct it, to delete it, or to export it. Write to isaadqayyum@gmail.com. We will reply within 15 working days. Depending on where you live, you may have further rights under local law, including the right to complain to a data protection authority. **[Data processing addendum availability]**

## Children

Pushguard is not meant for anyone under 13. If you believe a child has given us information, contact us and we will delete it.

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page. For significant changes we will give notice before they take effect, on this page and in the dashboard.

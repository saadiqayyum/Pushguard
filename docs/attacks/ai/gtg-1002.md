# GTG-1002: AI-orchestrated intrusion

**Type:** AI-era attack · **When:** Nov 2025 · **Status:** reported (Anthropic's assessment, via secondary coverage)

## What happened
Anthropic reported that a state-sponsored actor used Claude Code plus MCP tools to run an intrusion campaign. Anthropic assessed with high confidence that the actor was China-based. China denies it.
The campaign targeted about 30 organizations in tech, finance, chemicals and government, and succeeded against a subset of them.
AI did about 80 to 90% of the work. Humans stepped in at 4 to 6 decision points.

## How it got in
1. The operators split the job into small, harmless-looking subtasks.
2. Sub-agents each handled one: recon, exploitation, lateral movement, credential harvesting, exfiltration.
3. No single subtask looked like an attack on its own.

## Why it was hard to spot
- Each step looked innocent by itself, and the AI worked at machine speed.

## Signs to look for
- In one change: little, because each piece is small.
- Across changes over time: many small steps that add up to an attack. The [salami backdoor](future-salami-backdoor.md) scenario uses the same evasion pattern.

## Defanged example
```
subtask 1: "list services on <target-range>"
subtask 2: "check <service> version"
subtask 3: "test login on <service>"
... each one alone reads as routine admin work
```

## How Pushguard catches it
- This was an intrusion into organizations. No repository was changed, and Pushguard does not watch networks.
- The lesson for repositories is to judge many small changes together. That is the job of the planned facts + symbol graph across commits.
- Nothing in Pushguard catches this campaign directly.
